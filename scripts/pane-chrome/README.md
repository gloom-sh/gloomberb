# Pane chrome checks

[Back to CONTRIBUTING](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#pane-chrome-checks)

Unit tests press a pane's header with synthetic events, which is how v0.16.1 shipped panes that only moved from the grip and the title. `scripts/pane-chrome` drives the real desktop shell in headless Chrome with real pointer input instead, over a fixed layout of stand-in panes (`fixture.tsx`) with no network, and checks where each pane ends up: drags by the grip, the title and the bare header bar for docked, tabbed, failed, floating and fullscreen panes, tab select and reorder, the resize corner and edges, a dock divider and a table's sidebar divider (that the frames follow the pointer while it is still down, as well as where they land, and that a chart or heat map follows its frame before its content catches up while the table under a board's chart band keeps its size, and that the market heat map's board of 500 names holds every tile still through a corner drag with a refresh in it, a window resize and a maximise and restore, and is laid out once when the size settles, not on every move), a popped-out window's title bar and caption buttons (the native window bridge is a recorder), that a chart filling its box takes the pointer as before (the crosshair, a pan, a wheel zoom, the trend line and the ruler, for charts under a query bar, beside a table and under a toolbar), that a chart's time axis stays clear of a floating pane's resize grip, that a chart's fill at rest moves nothing outside it, that a chart composer on invented bars pans back without jumping or changing span, loading older bars as it goes and stopping at the oldest, and reads out every series under its crosshair in the legend and on the axes with no box floating over the plot, that a daily and a 30-minute chart composer in one tab follow each other's crosshair to the same session and clear with it, and so do IV history, a monthly macro series and Fear & Greed's charts beside a chart composer, that interface text cannot be selected (Ctrl+A, Select All, a double or triple click and a drag across a chart pane and docked panes select nothing) while reader and markdown text, a chat message and a field still can, that a layout at rest renders nothing, and that keys held down (real auto-repeat key events) copy one screenshot and close one pane while the arrow keys keep repeating, and the chat message list over an invented channel (`chat-fixture.tsx`): which row is marked after a notification opens on it, a hover, a scroll, a key or a new message, and where the list is after a message arrives, an older page loads above, a message above the view changes, the composer takes focus or grows, and the pane is narrowed. Steps whose scenario says `anchoring=off` run with the browser's own scroll anchoring switched off, as it is in browsers that have none.

The Pane chrome workflow runs these pointer checks on pull requests that touch the paths listed under `pull_request` in `.github/workflows/pane-chrome.yml`, and on pushes to `main` that touch them; a failed step fails the pull request. The Performance workflow's Pane drag frame budget job compares pane drags and resizes against the base branch for the same paths and for `performance.yml` itself: its Pane drag scope job reads the list (`scripts/pane-chrome/scope.ts`) and skips the budget in seconds for any other change.

- The layout steps (`layout-checks.ts`, panes in `layout-fixture.tsx`) open the detail reader through the real Trials and Changelog panes, the article reader, markdown, prose, a table, a page with a table as tall as its rows and label and value rows, a chart sized in rows under a query bar, and a series list beside its detail under a query bar, at 1280 by 720, 1440 by 900 and 720 by 900, docked and floating, and fail when content stops more than a pixel above the footer, text wraps or is cut short of the width it sits in, or a box or chart cuts off a few pixels of what it draws. `bun run check:pane-layout` measures every function in the catalog the same way on the real web build (`bun run web:build` first, or `--web <build>`), signed out, with its detail and, docked, every view its tabs and query bar choices lead to (two presses deep by default, `--depth` for more), each confirmed selected before it is measured (`tab-visits.ts`), and writes a summary, a coverage list of every tab (measured, not measured with the reason, or skipped as disabled, hidden or too deep) and a picture of each view; a tab it could not open fails the sweep (the `layout sweep:` step checks the tab visits themselves on `tab-sweep-fixture.tsx`: 25 tabs in an overflowing strip, a repeated label, a press that does nothing); text that arrived already cut in an answer it served (a feed's own shortened headline) is reported apart, not as a failure.
- The top bar steps (`header-checks.ts`, the bar alone in `header-fixture.tsx`) put the app's top bar in every update state (checking, available, downloading, installing, ready with its Restart button, failed with its retry key, a passing notice), with the command bar closed and open, at 520 to 1440 px wide, as the macOS title bar (also in full screen and at the largest text size), the Windows one with its caption buttons, the Linux one and the web app draw it, and fail when two of its items paint over each other or anything is cut by its slot or the window edge rather than ending in an ellipsis of its own. At the narrowest width (800 px at the largest text size) a real click on Restart restarts and one on Help opens Help.
- `bun run audit:ui` measures the whole app's look on its own copy of the web build: every function, the panes of the bundled plugins (and of the registry's with `--registry`), each one's detail and tabs, and the shell (header, status bar, command bar, dialogs, a pane menu, the first-run guide), at 640 and 1280 px. For every view it records each run of text (size, line height, weight, colour, its inset from the pane edge and the component that drew it), every element of a design-system family (buttons, tabs, chips, fields, table and list rows, headings, cards, empty, loading, notice and locked states, pane chrome, dialogs, menus, chart chrome) by shape, and row heights, and pictures each variant and every view in the white and paper themes. `scripts/pane-chrome/ui-audit-report.ts <run folder> --sheets` turns a run into a report of the variants per family with a picture grid each, the type and insets in use, colours against the theme and a scan of the code for colours, sizes, spacing and radii written as literals. `bun run audit:ui:compare --before <run> --after <run> [--sheets]` sets two runs side by side: variants per family, font sizes, weights, radii, shadows, rule colours and pane insets before and after, and with `--sheets` a pixel diff of every view's picture and a sheet per family of the views that moved, before over after, beside views that did not.
- `bun run kit:page` serves the kit page (`kit-fixture.tsx`): every family of the design system (../../docs/pane-conventions.md, "Design system") in every state, drawn by the shared components; `--shots <dir>` pictures each family in the white and paper themes at 640 and 1280 px, with hover and focus forced. Compare a UI change against it. The `readers:` step measures its readers in the browser: the article reader, markdown and Docs must set paragraphs, section headings and prose bullets alike, wide and in a narrow column. `src/architecture/ui-literals.test.ts` holds type sizes, weights, radii, drop shadows, greys and pixel spacing written out instead of taken from the tokens to `src/architecture/ui-literals-allowlist.json`, where each file says why it keeps them.
- `bun run check:ui-tokens` holds every view (the audit's, offline on a kept data snapshot) to the type scale, the two weights, the radii and the pane inset, with exceptions in `src/architecture/ui-tokens-allowlist.json`; the UI tokens workflow runs it on every pull request in eight shards and keeps the week's snapshot from main. `bun run ui:benchmark` pictures every view on a base and on this checkout and writes "N of M views changed" with the sheets of the views that moved; the UI benchmark workflow runs it on UI pull requests and keeps one comment up to date. See [Holding the line](../../docs/pane-conventions.md#holding-the-line).
- `bun run check:call-player` plays the Calls pane's invented call (`scenario=layout&pane=calls`) in headless Chromium and WebKit from a recording it makes with ffmpeg and serves in byte ranges, as storage does: a clicked paragraph plays from its start and is marked, the arrows seek and the mark follows, Space pauses, the speed and the track work, Cmd/Ctrl+F reaches the find and Enter steps its matches, and a call without a recording draws no player. It pictures the pane in the white and paper themes at 640 and 1280 px. It needs ffmpeg and playwright-core's Chromium and WebKit.
- `bun run check:chart-axis-zoom` zooms the chart composer's fixture (`scenario=cmp&chart=readout`: price, volume and RSI panels) with the real pointer in headless Chromium and WebKit: a drag on the price axis and a scroll on the RSI axis scale those panels alone and show Auto, a drag on the plot moves hand-scaled values, a double-click on the axis and Auto fit them again, a drag on the time axis zooms time from the newest bar and a double-click returns it, a pinch (Chromium's own, WebKit's gesture events) zooms time around the pointer, and a drag on the plot still pans time. It pictures the chart before, scaled and reset. It needs playwright-core's Chromium and WebKit.
- `bun run check:chart-panel-layout [--base <main checkout>]` lays out the same fixture with the real pointer in headless Chromium and WebKit at 640 and 1280 px: at rest it draws as the base does and shows none of the new controls, the tool bar's fold button and the legend's chevron show under the pointer and fold them to a button and a count that open them again (as Shift+T and Shift+L do), a legend entry still hides its series, a tool picked from the keys opens a hidden bar whose chevron folds it again for good, the line between two panels shows under the pointer, keeps the split under a click that jitters, moves with a drag and returns on a double-click, Shift+Up and Shift+Down move the top panel a row, a double-click on a panel gives it the whole chart and its `All panels` button (clicked or double-clicked) or another double-click gives the others back, and a drag on the plot still pans. It pictures each state and writes a contact sheet per browser. It needs playwright-core's Chromium and WebKit and ImageMagick.
- `bun run check:pane-chrome` runs every step once; `--only <text>` picks steps by name, `--repeat <n>` runs them again, `--root <checkout>` checks another checkout's sources. A failed step saves a screenshot under `~/.cache/gloomberb-pane-chrome/failures`.
- `bun run check:pane-rest --base <main checkout>` draws every fixture layout from both checkouts at 1440 by 900 and compares them pixel by pixel: how many pixels differ, in which pane and where, with the base, head and diff images saved under the cache folder. A difference is not a failure by itself; a change that draws a pane differently at rest says why in its PR.
- `bun run benchmark:pane-drag:compare --base <main checkout>` compares main-thread time per frame and React commits while dragging a docked and a floating pane (and a floating one at a hand's speed: speeding up and slowing down across the docked panes' edges, with a rest), resizing a floating news table, chart, heat map (an invented one, and the market board of 500 names recoloured by ticks) and Fear & Greed pane by the corner, the same for the charts that sit inside a pane's own layout (under a query bar, over a table, beside a table, under a toolbar), dragging a dock divider (measured on the news table's side and the chart's, and between the docked G layout and a chart beside a table), a table's sidebar divider, and panning a chart composer's plot. It reports frames per second, the 95th percentile frame time and estimated missed 60 Hz frames (requestAnimationFrame timing, not frames a GPU presented), and for a resize how far the frame and its content fell behind the pointer and moved on release, and for a chart how far CSS scaled its bitmap during the drag and at rest; a frame or its content more than three cells behind, moving more than a cell on release, or a chart not drawn 1:1 at rest fails it. For a docked or floating pane drag it also counts the frames where the pane (or the outline a docked one drags) stood still while the pointer moved, and where it moved more than 2 px differently from the pointer; either one growing by half over the base and by four frames fails it, and so do a 95th percentile frame over 25 ms, more than six missed frames, or, in the hand-speed drag, a frame where the pane moved more than 6 px differently from the pointer (a magnet's jump), where the base draws at 60 Hz. `--only <text>` picks scenarios by name. Both sides draw the fixture of this checkout; when it imports source the base does not have, the base is built from its own fixture and the job summary says so (a base with no fixture, or one that does not build, fails the job).
- `bun run benchmark:pane-drag:smoothness --side <label>=<checkout> ...` drags a floating pane around and a docked pane out of the dock with the real pointer, as a hand does (`--speed slow,medium,fast`: 150, 450 and 1,300 px/s between stops, speeding up and slowing down, resting at stops a few px from other panes' edges, where the magnets catch; `sweep` is one 2 s loop), in the web build (`--app web`: the workspace a first visit gets, with the watchlist's ticker floating, over the reads `web-app.ts` replays) and the desktop view's fixture (`--app desktop`), in Chromium and WebKit (`--engine`). For each checkout it reports the frame time (50th and 95th percentile), dropped 60 Hz frames, long tasks, main-thread time per frame, React commits per pointer move, the frames drawn late with the commits and quote frames that came in them (and in Chromium the scripts of its long animation frames), and how the moving pane kept to the pointer, with the largest jump off the pointer's motion in one frame. `--stream live` streams quotes from Gloom Cloud into the web build as the web app does, signed out; `--record-stream <s> --replay-dir <dir>` keeps them, with the reads, and `--stream replay --replay-dir <dir>` plays them to every checkout at the pace they came. `--gpu` runs Chrome with its GPU instead of the headless shell, `--shots <dir>` pictures each drag at rest at each stop and after the release, `--trace <dir>` writes a Chromium performance trace of each drag and `--profile <dir>` a CPU profile, with the functions that took the most time printed. On a Mac whose display sleeps, headless Chrome paints only 8 to 30 frames a second, so read the frame times from WebKit or Linux there; Chrome's predicted pointer (which the host draws a drag at) is not predicted for synthetic input, so only a real mouse shows it.
- The first run downloads the pinned Chrome for Testing headless shell (`browser.ts`); `CHROME_PATH` uses another Chrome instead.
- To add a case, add a step to `checks.ts` that starts from a fixture scenario, moves the pointer with `drag` or `click`, and checks the result in the page (where a pane is, what is selected), not which handler ran. A new kind of pane or layout goes in `fixture.tsx`.
- Theme/paint checks use `--only "theme preview"` or `--only "first paint"`. The real picker previews every built-in palette over chart, overview, heat map, table, sparkline, glyph and 3D surfaces, including floating and detached chrome. Each animation frame checks pane and plot backgrounds; settled pixels and color tokens must agree before/after commit and cancellation. Cold opens and rapid chart/overview switches must never expose an empty plot or change its size after first paint; the tabs stay mounted and hidden once opened, as the research pane's do, and a tab that comes back must show a bitmap drawn for the box it shows in, on every frame; the research Chart tab (the chart composer on invented bars) and a newly added chart pane, cold or drawn from cached bars, must paint the chart they settle on from their first plotted frame: same plot size and place, same axis and legend labels. Each frame is sampled after it painted (a task posted from its animation callback), so what resize observers change in that frame counts. Samples go to `paint/` under the cache folder. Theme changes finish color-only CSS transitions before paint; hover and layout motion keep their usual timing otherwise.
- Not covered: GPU compositing (the runner has none) and the native macOS WebKit view; the desktop window move is checked up to the bridge message. The macOS view reads only the `-webkit-` spelling of `user-select`, which Chrome cannot tell apart from the plain one, so `src/renderers/dom/selection-style.test.ts` holds the stylesheet and the host to writing both.
