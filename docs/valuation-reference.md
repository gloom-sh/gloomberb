# Market valuation reference

`VAL` compares broad valuation and allocation measures with their loaded histories. Each detail shows available dollar levels and comparison dates, with current data status in the pane footer. Formulas, source references and methodology are documented here. The table's valuation zones are fixed application thresholds, not investment recommendations or forecasts.

## Source coverage and units

| Measure | Configured inputs and calculation | Interpretation and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buffett indicator | (`NCBEILQ027S` + `FBCELLQ027S`) / nominal GDP × 100. The numerator is the Z.1 market value of corporate equities issued by nonfinancial corporate business and by domestic financial sectors, quarterly end-of-period, USD millions scaled to billions. GDP is a quarterly, seasonally adjusted annual rate in USD billions. | This is the Federal Reserve's own dollar market value of US corporate equity, not an index level. It includes closely held equity and excludes foreign issuers listed in the US, so it is not the same universe as a total-market index. It moves in quarterly steps and revises with each Z.1 release; the latest quarter lags about ten weeks. [Nonfinancial metadata](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NCBEILQ027S), [financial metadata](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/FBCELLQ027S), [GDP metadata](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GDP). |
| Tobin's Q | `NCBEILQ027S` / `TNWMVBSNNCB`: nonfinancial corporate equity liabilities divided by net worth. Both quarterly, end-of-period Z.1 series are in USD millions and are scaled to billions before calculation. | This implementation is an equity-to-net-worth proxy. Its line at 1 means equal equity and net-worth levels; it does not separately measure replacement cost. These sector aggregates should not be described as a listed-stock portfolio. [Equity metadata](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NCBEILQ027S), [net-worth metadata](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TNWMVBSNNCB). |

| Household equity allocation | `BOGZ1FL153064486Q`, already expressed as a percentage. | Includes households and nonprofit organizations, and both direct and indirect corporate-equity holdings as a share of financial assets. ## The market-capitalization numerator

The three market-cap ratios share one numerator: the sum of two Z.1 series on their common quarter-end dates. A quarter that only one leg has reported is not an observation. Earlier revisions used the `^W5000` price history as if one index point were one billion dollars. Wilshire's divisor adjustments change that conversion over time, so the pane treated those ratios as unavailable until a monetary series replaced the index level. [Wilshire Index Rule Book, sections 2.2.1 and 6.2](https://assets-global.website-files.com/60f8038183eb84c40e8c14e9/612f894c20ad970151859d9d_FT%20Wilshire%205000%20Index%20Series.pdf).

The Z.1 numerator is a different universe from a listed-market index. It counts every US corporation's equity at market value, including closely held companies, and omits foreign-domiciled issuers that trade in the US. It therefore runs well above the index-based Buffett indicator most published charts show: about 279% against roughly 200% at the 2021 peak, and 85% against roughly 57% at the 2009 trough. Compare the pane's reading with its own history and bands, not with an index-based figure. The bands for the three ratios are anchored on the Z.1 series' own distribution (Buffett on 1947-2026; cap/profits and cap/M2 on 1989-2026, since earlier decades would place the whole modern era in one band). The quarterly cadence also means the latest reading can be ten weeks old while an index-based figure is daily.

## Dates and historical comparisons

Ratio histories align on numerator observation dates. The denominator is interpolated between observations and held flat after its latest observation; numerator dates before the first denominator observation are omitted. An explicit missing or invalid denominator observation interrupts interpolation and carry until another usable observation; a nonpositive denominator cannot define these ratios. Invalid calendar dates are rejected before replacing usable cached observations. An entirely unavailable selected measure keeps its identity and error instead of switching to another indicator. Known gaps remain dated chart gaps and leave current value, zone and rank unavailable, while valid historical statistics remain usable. Missing numerator observations likewise remain gaps; a reported zero numerator and signed excess yields remain numeric. Shiller's columns publish on different lags: price runs to the current month while dividends trail it by about a quarter. Blank months at the end of a Shiller column are treated as not yet reported, so the dividend yield ends at, and is dated by, its last reported dividend month; its age threshold allows for that lag. This is a historical alignment convention, not a reconstruction of what was publicly available on each past date. FRED data can be revised, and interpolated values can use later observations.

For monetary ratios, the `as of …Q…` label beside the denominator identifies its latest observation quarter. It is not a publication date or an unrevised data vintage. The pane footer identifies the selected measure's latest observation and flags data that exceed that measure's age threshold. Refresh time alone does not establish that the underlying observation is current. Manual refresh bypasses the six-hour series cache; normal reads reuse it. Concurrent Shiller columns share only the pending request. Failed refreshes retain usable cached observations with their original retrieval times and active errors, including later cache reads, until a successful fetch. Chart warnings retain active source failures and gaps. Headless source metadata retains each leg’s local retrieval time and fallback status, with the upstream provider’s retrieval time and stale declaration kept separately under `provider` through cache reads and restarts; its aggregate retrieval time is the oldest known leg time, or unavailable when any leg time is unknown.

`1Y ago` uses the last available observation on or before 365 days before the latest value. Mean, high, low, percentile and trend statistics use the entire loaded history. The selected range changes the displayed chart window; it does not redefine those statistics or expand to older history when fewer than two usable observations remain. “All-time” extrema refer to the available input history and retain their observation dates.

## Zones, yields and trends

The color bands use fixed thresholds for each measure. The `RICH` percentile and trend deviation orient values so a larger number means more expensive: lower yields and excess yields reverse the direction used by price ratios. This normalization supports comparison; the underlying measures retain different economic meanings.

Trend fits are log-linear for positive level measures and linear for excess CAPE yield, which can be zero or negative. The deviation is measured against the fitted history, not against an independently estimated fair value. Economic regimes, interest rates, sector composition, revisions and source coverage can all affect historical comparisons. Allocation and receivables measures describe investor positioning and balance sheets rather than directly pricing future cash flows.
