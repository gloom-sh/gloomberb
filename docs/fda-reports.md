# FDA reports and recalls (FDA)

`FDA` searches three public FDA datasets, one at a time: drug adverse event reports (FAERS), device adverse event reports (MAUDE) and drug recalls from the FDA enforcement report. The Dataset choice above the list switches between them, and each keeps its own order, newest first by the date FDA received the report or the recall began. Beside it are the filters each dataset offers: drug reports by outcome (death, serious, not serious) and by the role of the drug the row names, device reports by event type (death, injury, malfunction), recalls by class and status, and every dataset by how far back to look (Range). The figure above the list is how many records of that dataset match the search and filters, with the date FDA last updated the dataset; drug reports are published about once a quarter, so the newest are weeks or months old. More records load as you scroll, up to the 25,050 that FDA's interface lets anyone page through, and the pane warns when a search goes past that.

An adverse event report says that someone (a patient, a doctor, a manufacturer) saw a health problem while a drug or device was in use. It does not establish that the product caused it: reports are voluntary, unverified, often duplicated, and counts depend on how widely a product is used and how much attention it gets, so they cannot be read as rates or risks. A death outcome means the patient died, not that the product was the cause. Each drug in a report carries the reporter's role for it: suspect, concomitant (taken at the same time) or interacting. The list names the drug the search matched, with that role, and the detail lists every drug in the report. FDA cannot tie a role to one drug in a search, so the Role filter keeps the reports where the drug the search matched plays that part, checked report by report; the figure above the list then shows only the data date, since FDA's count would include reports where the drug only came along. The Labeler column names the firm when a single one markets the product and counts them for a generic, whose record lists every company that labels it; products FDA's product data does not cover, most vaccines among them, have no labeler, and the column is left out when no row has one. Device reports name the device the search matched, its manufacturer and the device problems reported. Recalls carry FDA's classification (Class I is the most serious, Class III the least), their status and, on a wide pane, the reason. The worst outcomes are coloured: a death or a Class I recall in red, a serious report, an injury, a Class II recall or one still ongoing in amber; a drug that was only taken alongside the suspect one is dimmed. The words are always there, so nothing depends on the colour.

The search matches names as a phrase: drug reports by drug name (as reported, brand or generic), device reports by device brand, generic name or manufacturer, and recalls by firm, product description or reason. Drug reports are not searched by company, because their manufacturer field lists every firm that labels the product, dozens for a generic, so a company name would match reports of other firms' drugs; search a company's drugs by name, or its recalls and devices by its name. Nothing is matched to tickers: a firm is found by the name it files under, which can differ from its trading name and leaves out subsidiaries that file under their own. `o` opens the record itself.

Without a key, FDA allows each address 240 requests a minute and 1,000 a day; the pane sends one request per search or page, and a few more when the Role filter with a search leaves a page short. Setting `OPENFDA_API_KEY` in the environment before starting the terminal app raises those limits; the key is sent in a request header and is not stored or shown.
