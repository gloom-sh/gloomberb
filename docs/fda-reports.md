# FDA reports and recalls (FDA)

`FDA` searches three public FDA datasets, one at a time: drug adverse event reports (FAERS), device adverse event reports (MAUDE) and drug recalls from the FDA enforcement report. The Dataset choice above the list switches between them, and each keeps its own order, newest first by the date FDA received the report or the recall began. The figure above the list is how many records of that dataset match the search, with the date FDA last updated the dataset; drug reports are published about once a quarter, so the newest are weeks or months old. More records load as you scroll, up to the 25,050 that FDA's interface lets anyone page through, and the pane warns when a search goes past that.

An adverse event report says that someone (a patient, a doctor, a manufacturer) saw a health problem while a drug or device was in use. It does not establish that the product caused it: reports are voluntary, unverified, often duplicated, and counts depend on how widely a product is used and how much attention it gets, so they cannot be read as rates or risks. A death outcome means the patient died, not that the product was the cause. Each drug in a report carries the reporter's role for it: suspect, concomitant (taken at the same time) or interacting. The list names the drug the search matched, with that role, and the detail lists every drug in the report. The labeler is shown only when a single firm markets the product, because a generic drug's record lists every company that labels it. Device reports name the device the search matched and its manufacturer. Recalls carry FDA's classification: Class I is the most serious, Class III the least.

The search matches names as a phrase: drug reports by drug name (as reported, brand or generic), device reports by device brand, generic name or manufacturer, and recalls by firm, product description or reason. Drug reports are not searched by company, because their manufacturer field lists every firm that labels the product, dozens for a generic, so a company name would match reports of other firms' drugs; search a company's drugs by name, or its recalls and devices by its name. Nothing is matched to tickers: a firm is found by the name it files under, which can differ from its trading name and leaves out subsidiaries that file under their own. `o` opens the record itself.

Without a key, FDA allows each address 240 requests a minute and 1,000 a day; the pane sends one request per search or page. Setting `OPENFDA_API_KEY` in the environment before starting the terminal app raises those limits; the key is sent in a request header and is not stored or shown.
